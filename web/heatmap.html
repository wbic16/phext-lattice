Heat map

Every scroll of a phext is a point of light in the subspace void. The full text at each scroll colours its node — gold, white, or blue by the text itself — and the fabric woven between neighbouring scrolls takes its colour from the text at both ends. The whole eleven-dimensional space (nine address numbers, plus each scroll's own page of lines and columns) is projected down to three dimensions, or two. Search, and the view rebuilds itself around the scrolls that match.

How the projection works

A scroll's address has nine numbers — library.shelf.series / collection.volume.book / chapter.section.scroll — and its page adds two more dimensions, its lines and its columns. The projection folds the nine address numbers into three axes with log scales (each number v contributes ln(1+v), so wide ranges stay readable):

x = ln(1+chapter) + 0.7·ln(1+collection) + 0.45·ln(1+library)
y = ln(1+section) + 0.7·ln(1+volume) + 0.45·ln(1+shelf)
z = ln(1+scroll) + 0.7·ln(1+book) + 0.45·ln(1+series)

Each axis is then stretched across the view by its 2nd–98th percentile range, so the dense heart of the phext fills the sky and the rare extremes rest at the edges. The page dimensions ride on the node itself: a scroll's line count sets its node's height, its widest line its width. The text sets the colour — a hash of the scroll's full text picks a tone on the gold–white–blue line — and its size sets the brightness. The fabric joins each scroll to the next scroll, the next section, and the next chapter, drawn as bezier curves whose two halves carry the colours of the scrolls they join. In 2D the same fold is drawn flat: x across, y up, and z carried by brightness. Nothing here is random: the same phext always draws the same sky.

Very large phexts (over 20,000 scrolls) draw their 20,000 largest scrolls as nodes — by text size, or by match count while searching — and fold the rest into the ambient glow; the page notes how many were folded.
